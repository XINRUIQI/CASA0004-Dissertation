# P103 — Agerton & Upton (2019)

## Full reference

Agerton, M. and Upton, G.B. (2019) 'Decomposing Crude Price Differentials: Domestic Shipping Constraints or the Crude Oil Export Ban?', *The Energy Journal*, 40(3), pp. 155–172.

- **DOI**: [10.5547/01956574.40.3.mage](https://doi.org/10.5547/01956574.40.3.mage)
- **Research type**: Price-differential decomposition / policy analysis (not forecasting)
- **Project ID**: P103 — M1 `crude_exports` 制度断点支撑

## Research topic

What drives US crude **location basis differentials** — domestic shipping and pipeline constraints vs the **US crude oil export ban** (EPCA, largely in force until December 2015)?

## Data

- US crude spot price differentials (including WTI–Brent and domestic locational spreads)
- Shipping / pipeline cost proxies
- Regime spanning pre- and post-export-ban lifting (December 2015)

## Method

Decomposition/regression framework attributing components of crude price differentials to transportation frictions versus the export ban; examines whether lifting the ban restored arbitrage and narrowed spreads.

## Key findings

1. Both **domestic shipping constraints** and the **export ban** contributed to elevated US crude price differentials.
2. The export ban prevented spatial arbitrage that would otherwise move surplus US crude to global markets.
3. After the ban was lifted, evidence supports **narrower spreads** and restored arbitrage incentives, though sample length post-2015 limits formal cointegration tests in some follow-on work.

## Relevance to this project (M1)

- Direct literature anchor for **`crude_exports` regime break** in `m1_data_dictionary.md` §6: pre-2016 low export volumes reflect **policy binding**, not full market signal.

- Comparison window 2019–2025 lies entirely in the post-ban regime.
- ⚠️ **Not** evidence that `crude_exports` significantly improves weekly Brent ML forecasts out-of-sample.
