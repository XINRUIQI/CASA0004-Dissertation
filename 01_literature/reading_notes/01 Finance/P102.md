# P102 — Büyükşahin, Lee, Moser & Robe (2013)

## Full reference

Büyükşahin, B., Lee, T.K., Moser, J.T. and Robe, M.A. (2013) 'Physical Markets, Paper Markets and the WTI-Brent Spread', *The Energy Journal*, 34(3), pp. 129–152.

- **DOI**: [10.5547/01956574.34.3.7](https://doi.org/10.5547/01956574.34.3.7)
- **Research type**: Market structure / spread mechanism (not forecasting)
- **Project ID**: P102 — M1 `brent_wti_spread` 机制支撑

## Research topic

How physical crude oil markets (inventories, pipelines, regional spot prices) and paper markets (futures, spread trading) jointly determine the WTI–Brent price differential, especially during the 2010–2012 period when the spread widened dramatically.

## Data

- WTI and Brent spot and futures prices (daily)
- US inland inventory and transportation constraint context (Cushing bottleneck era)
- Period centred on post-2010 spread divergence

## Method

Structural/econometric analysis linking physical-market frictions and financial-market arbitrage to the WTI–Brent spread; distinguishes constraints in physical delivery from paper-market pricing.

## Key findings

1. The WTI–Brent spread reflects **both** physical market bottlenecks and paper-market dynamics—not futures prices alone.
2. During the shale boom / Cushing congestion period, spatial arbitrage was impaired, contributing to persistent WTI discount vs Brent.
3. Physical and paper markets can decouple when transportation or policy barriers block crude flows.

## Relevance to this project (M1)

- Supports including **`brent_wti_spread`** as an economically meaningful cross-market state variable.
- Complements **P103** (export ban decomposition) and **P112** Scheitrum et al. (2018) on the 2011 pricing-structure break.
- ⚠️ **Not** evidence that the spread improves weekly Brent **out-of-sample** forecasts — use for interpretation and regime context only.
