# Reading Note — Paper P088: TransES-ETA for Vessel Arrival-Time Prediction

## Citation

Anonymous authors. (2026). *TransES-ETA: A Novel Transformer-Based Explainable and Efficient Structure for Predicting Estimated Time of Vessel Arrival*. Conference paper under double-blind review at ICLR 2026.

- **Status**: Under review at ICLR 2026
- **Authors**: Anonymous due to double-blind review
- **DOI / arXiv**: Not provided in the manuscript
- **Research domain**: Maritime artificial intelligence, AIS trajectory modelling, vessel ETA prediction

---

## Core Method

- **Model**: TransES-ETA, an end-to-end Transformer-based multi-task learning framework.
- **Primary target**: The vessel's remaining travel time to the final destination port, measured in hours.
- **Auxiliary target**: The vessel's next port of call, including intermediate calling ports and the final destination.
- **Input data**: Automatic Identification System (AIS) vessel trajectories, containing spatial position, speed, heading, time intervals, departure port, and vessel type.
- **Data representation**: Raw AIS trajectories are divided into consecutive spatial grids, creating a nested sequence with:
  - An outer sequence of spatial grids.
  - An inner set of AIS observations within each grid.
- **Spatial encoding**: Transformer-inspired spatial encoding is used to convert grid-centre longitude and latitude into high-dimensional vectors.
- **Local-pattern encoder**: A Graph Convolutional Network with Residual Connections (GCNwRC) represents the local AIS movement patterns within each grid.
- **Time encoding**: Modified sinusoidal encoding represents irregular time intervals between AIS observations.
- **Categorical encoding**: Learnable embeddings represent departure ports and vessel types.
- **Port-call branch**: Average pooling and a bidirectional GRU are used to predict the next port and generate route-related latent features.
- **ETA branch**: A Transformer Encoder fuses the AIS representation and port-call representation before producing the ETA regression result.
- **Feature reconstruction**: A Transformer Decoder reconstructs the input features to reduce information loss in the encoded representation.
- **Additional operational information**: Port turnaround time, or port-stay time, is added to the predicted sailing time to obtain the final ETA.
- **Training strategy**: Port classification loss, ETA regression loss, and feature-reconstruction loss are jointly optimised through dynamic multi-task weighting.
- **Evaluation**:
  - Accuracy for port-call prediction.
  - Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for ETA prediction.
  - Mean Squared Error (MSE) for ETA prediction.
  - Training and inference time for computational efficiency.

---

## Dataset

- **Period**: 2024.
- **Data type**: Real-world AIS vessel trajectory data.
- **Vessel categories**:
  - Crude oil tankers.
  - Container ships.
- **Geographical coverage**:
  - South China Sea.
  - East China Sea.
  - Yellow Sea.
  - Other connected maritime areas in East and Southeast Asia.
- **Departure ports**: 34 ports located mainly in China, South Korea, and Japan.
- **Departure and calling ports combined**: 51 ports.
- **Final destination**: Port of Singapore.
- **Average voyage distance**: 2,467.94 nautical miles, approximately 4,570.62 kilometres.
- **Total duration of all voyages**: Approximately 45,034.35 hours.
- **Route characteristics**: All trajectories include intermediate calling ports, causing routes to change dynamically between departure and destination.
- **Sampling strategy**: Sliding-window sampling.
- **Window specification**:
  - Ten consecutive spatial grids per sample.
  - Each grid covers approximately 6 × 6 nautical miles.
- **Train–test split**: 90% training data and 10% testing data.

## Semantic Data Transformation

The paper argues that raw AIS data cannot be treated as an ordinary regularly sampled time series because it contains:

- Irregular reporting intervals.
- Missing and noisy observations.
- Unequal numbers of AIS points across spatial areas.
- Complex local movement behaviour.
- Long-distance dependencies across complete voyages.

The authors therefore divide each vessel trajectory into consecutive spatial grids.

Each grid contains:

- A grid-centre coordinate.
- A variable-length set of AIS observations.
- A time-distance value.
- Associated departure-port and vessel-type information.

The resulting nested structure can be represented as:

```text
Voyage trajectory
├── Spatial grid 1
│   ├── AIS observation 1
│   ├── AIS observation 2
│   └── AIS observation 3
├── Spatial grid 2
│   ├── AIS observation 4
│   └── AIS observation 5
└── Spatial grid N
```

The outer sequence represents the long-distance vessel route, while the inner AIS observations represent local movement patterns within each grid.

---

## GCNwRC Local-Pattern Encoder

The paper replaces the GRU-based local-pattern encoder used in previous work with a Graph Convolutional Network with Residual Connections.

Within each spatial grid:

- Each AIS observation is treated as a graph node.
- The AIS observations are organised as a directed or bidirectional complete graph.
- All observations within the same grid are connected.
- Graphs belonging to different grids remain disconnected at this stage.
- The Transformer later models relationships across grids.

The GCNwRC module contains:

- Two graph-convolution layers.
- Two batch-normalisation layers.
- ReLU activation functions.
- One dropout layer.
- A residual connection between the beginning and end of the module.

The main motivation is computational efficiency.

A GRU must process the local AIS sequence within each grid sequentially, limiting batch processing. The graph-based representation allows multiple within-grid point sets to be processed in parallel.

---

## Port-Call Prediction Branch

The auxiliary task predicts the next port that the vessel will reach.

The predicted port can be:

- An intermediate calling port.
- The final destination port.

The branch includes:

- Average pooling of the multi-channel AIS features.
- A bidirectional GRU for sequential feature extraction.
- A fully connected classification layer.
- Cross-entropy loss.

The Bi-GRU output is divided into two paths:

1. One path produces the next-port classification result.
2. The second path provides route-related latent features to the ETA branch.

The port-call task therefore has two functions:

- It provides an operationally interpretable intermediate prediction.
- It encourages the shared encoder to learn route and port-operation information that is useful for ETA prediction.

---

## ETA Prediction Branch

The ETA branch receives:

1. AIS sequence features from the semantic feature-extraction module.
2. Route and port-call features from the auxiliary port-prediction branch.

After feature fusion, the Transformer Encoder models:

- Long-range temporal dependencies.
- Relationships between different spatial grids.
- Interactions among movement patterns, vessel type, departure port, and port calls.
- Global dependencies that conventional RNN or CNN models may not fully capture.

A fully connected layer converts the Transformer representation into:

```text
Predicted remaining sailing time in hours
```

The final prediction is calculated as:

```text
Final ETA
=
Model-predicted remaining sailing time
+
Port turnaround time
```

---

## Feature-Reconstruction Task

A Transformer Decoder is placed after the Transformer Encoder.

The decoder attempts to reconstruct the original fused input features from the encoded latent representation.

The reconstruction objective encourages the encoder to:

- Preserve important information from the original AIS input.
- Avoid compressing the representation only around the ETA target.
- Learn a richer and more transferable representation.
- Reduce information loss during encoding.

The complete framework therefore optimises three tasks:

```text
1. Next-port classification
2. ETA regression
3. Input-feature reconstruction
```

---

## Multi-Task Loss

The total loss is defined as:

\[
L_{\text{total}}
=
\alpha L_{\text{port}}
+
0.001(0.66-\alpha)L_{\text{ETA}}
+
0.34L_{\text{reconstruction}}
\]

where:

- \(L_{\text{port}}\) is the port-call classification loss.
- \(L_{\text{ETA}}\) is the ETA regression loss.
- \(L_{\text{reconstruction}}\) is the feature-reconstruction loss.
- \(0 < \alpha \leq 0.33\).

The ETA loss is multiplied by 0.001 because its initial magnitude is substantially larger than those of the classification and reconstruction losses.

The model also uses dynamic task weighting:

- At the beginning of training, the port-prediction and ETA tasks are jointly learned.
- If the port-prediction loss does not decrease for ten epochs, the auxiliary task is treated as having largely converged.
- The relative weight assigned to ETA learning is then increased.
- This allows the auxiliary task to guide early representation learning without permanently dominating the main task.

## Key Findings

1. **TransES-ETA achieves strong ETA-prediction performance.**

   The complete model obtains:

   - MAE: approximately 2.46 hours.
   - MSE: approximately 14.51 hours².

2. **The port-call auxiliary task substantially improves ETA prediction.**

   Without the port-call module:

   - MAE: 5.39 hours.
   - MSE: 74.38 hours².

   With the port-call module:

   - MAE: 2.46 hours.
   - MSE: 14.51 hours².

   This represents approximately:

   - A 54.55% reduction in MAE.
   - An 80.49% reduction in MSE.

   The particularly large reduction in MSE suggests that the port-call task reduces both average errors and extreme ETA prediction errors.

3. **GCNwRC outperforms the original GRU-based local encoder.**

   Original GRU-based model:

   - MAE: 3.32 hours.
   - MSE: 30.83 hours².
   - Training time: 215 minutes.
   - Single-sample inference time: 0.0181 seconds.

   Improved GCNwRC model:

   - MAE: approximately 2.46 hours.
   - MSE: approximately 14.52 hours².
   - Training time: 41 minutes.
   - Single-sample inference time: 0.0165 seconds.

   Relative improvements include:

   - A 26.04% reduction in MAE.
   - A 52.90% reduction in MSE.
   - An 80.93% reduction in training time.
   - An 8.84% reduction in inference time.

4. **The port-call task provides structural explainability.**

   Rather than producing only an unexplained ETA value, the model also predicts the vessel's next port.

   The predicted port-call schedule provides an operational explanation for the final arrival-time estimate.

5. **Different AIS information types benefit from specialised encoders.**

   The paper demonstrates that:

   - Coordinates can be represented through spatial encoding.
   - Irregular time intervals can be represented through time encoding.
   - Port and vessel categories can be represented through embeddings.
   - Local AIS point sets can be represented through graph neural networks.
   - Long-distance trajectory dependencies can be represented through Transformers.

6. **Port turnaround time contains additional predictive information.**

   Explicitly incorporating port-stay time further improves ETA accuracy, although the port-call branch can implicitly learn part of this information from AIS trajectories.

---

## Main Contributions

### 1. Structured AIS Representation

The paper avoids reducing AIS trajectories to a limited number of static variables such as average speed or voyage distance.

Instead, it preserves:

- Spatial-grid structure.
- Within-grid vessel movement.
- Irregular temporal intervals.
- Port and vessel semantics.
- Long-distance route dependencies.

### 2. Semantics-Specific Encoders

Different input types are processed through different encoding methods rather than being directly concatenated as homogeneous numerical variables.

### 3. Multi-Task Learning

The port-call task acts as an operationally meaningful auxiliary objective that improves the representation used for ETA prediction.

### 4. Structural Explainability

The predicted next port provides an interpretable intermediate result connecting vessel-route behaviour with the final ETA.

### 5. Computational Efficiency

Replacing sequential GRU processing with graph-based batch processing substantially reduces training time.

## Implications for M1–M4

### M1: Market Fundamentals

The paper does not directly provide financial or oil-market variables for M1.

M1 should remain an interpretable structured baseline containing variables such as:

- Historical Brent and WTI prices.
- Oil inventories.
- OVX or VIX.
- Exchange rates.
- Interest rates.
- Production indicators.
- Demand indicators.

The shipping model should be evaluated by testing whether it adds predictive value beyond M1.

### M2: Remote-Sensing Extension

The paper supports the principle that each modality should use an encoder suited to its structure.

For this dissertation:

- Satellite imagery can use CNN, SatMAE, Prithvi, or another Earth-observation encoder.
- Shipping and AIS data can use graph and temporal encoders.
- Financial time series can use TCN, GRU, TFT, or Temporal Transformer.

The modality-specific representations should then be fused at an intermediate or late stage.

### M3: Shipping Extension

The paper is most directly relevant to M3.

It supports prioritising tanker-specific PortWatch variables such as:

```text
pw_hormuz_n_tanker

pw_suez_n_tanker
pw_suez_capacity_tanker
pw_malacca_n_tanker
pw_malacca_capacity_tanker
```

rather than relying exclusively on:

```text
total_vessel_count
total_vessel_capacity
```

It also suggests extending M3 beyond vessel counts and capacity to include:

- Average tanker speed.
- Tanker-speed anomalies.
- Anchorage waiting time.
- Port dwell time.
- Port turnaround time.
- ETA delays.
- Expected tanker arrivals.
- Route-deviation indicators.
- Rerouting shares.
- Origin–destination tanker flows.
- Chokepoint congestion.
- Port-call frequency.
- Vessel-type composition.

For M4, the paper supports a representation-level structure such as:

```text

        ↓

        ↓
```


## Suggested Adaptation of the Shipping Encoder

### Case A: Only Aggregated PortWatch Data Are Available

If the dissertation uses daily or weekly PortWatch indicators rather than vessel-level AIS trajectories, the original point-level GCNwRC should not be directly reproduced.

A more appropriate structure would be:

```text
Weekly chokepoint observations
[count, capacity, growth, anomaly, congestion]
        ↓
Chokepoint or port embedding
        ↓
Graph layer over the shipping network
GCN / GAT
        ↓
Temporal layer
TCN / GRU / Temporal Transformer
        ↓

```

Possible graph nodes include:

- Strait of Hormuz.
- Suez Canal.
- Bab el-Mandeb.
- Strait of Malacca.
- Cape of Good Hope.
- Major oil-export terminals.
- Major refinery and import ports.

Graph edges can represent major crude-oil shipping routes.

A closer adaptation of TransES-ETA would be:

```text
Vessel-level AIS observations
        ↓
Spatial partitioning by port, route segment, or chokepoint
        ↓
Local AIS point encoder

        ↓
Route-sequence encoder
Transformer / TCN
        ↓
Single-vessel representation
        ↓
Weekly attention pooling across vessels
        ↓
Weekly shipping representation z_ship
```

## Limitations

1. **The paper is still under review.**

   It is an anonymous ICLR 2026 submission rather than a formally accepted publication, so its findings should be treated as preliminary.

2. **Limited geographical generalisability.**

   The dataset mainly covers East Asian ports and voyages to Singapore. It does not evaluate:

   - Strait of Hormuz routes.
   - Red Sea routes.
   - Suez Canal routes.
   - European routes.
   - Atlantic Basin routes.
   - United States Gulf Coast routes.

3. **Single final destination.**

   All voyages end at Singapore, which simplifies the destination structure and may limit transferability to global shipping networks.

4. **Limited vessel categories.**

   The dataset includes crude oil tankers and container ships but excludes:

   - Product tankers.
   - LNG carriers.
   - LPG carriers.
   - Dry-bulk vessels.
   - Chemical tankers.

5. **No direct evidence for oil-price forecasting.**

   The paper predicts vessel ETA rather than Brent or WTI prices. It therefore cannot demonstrate that its AIS representation will necessarily improve commodity-price forecasts.

6. **Potentially weak out-of-sample design.**

   The paper reports a 90%/10% train–test split but does not clearly state whether the split is:

   - Chronological.
   - Random.
   - Vessel-independent.
   - Route-independent.
   - Port-independent.

   If overlapping windows from the same voyage occur in both training and testing sets, the reported performance may overestimate true generalisation.

7. **Limited information about dataset size.**

   The manuscript does not clearly report the total number of:

   - Voyages.
   - AIS messages.
   - Sliding-window samples.
   - Independent vessels.

8. **Cross-paper benchmark results are not fully comparable.**

   Previous studies use different datasets, vessel types, routes, and evaluation settings. Lower MAE values do not necessarily establish universal model superiority.

9. **Port turnaround time is partly handled outside the model.**

   Port-stay duration is added after ETA regression rather than consistently modelled as an input or separate prediction target.

10. **Interpretability remains partial.**

    Predicting the next port provides structural interpretability but does not fully explain:

    - Which AIS observations drive the prediction.
    - Which spatial grids are most important.
    - How speed, heading, and route deviation affect ETA.
    - Whether the learned relationships are causal.

11. **Limited comparison with simple baselines on the same dataset.**

    The paper does not provide a comprehensive same-dataset comparison with:

    - Linear regression.
    - Random forest.
    - XGBoost.
    - Historical-average ETA.
    - Route-specific mean ETA.

12. **Risk of excessive complexity for weekly oil-price data.**

    A weekly Brent dataset may contain only several hundred to approximately one thousand observations. Directly training a GCN–Bi-GRU–Transformer–Decoder architecture on such a small sample could lead to severe overfitting.

---

## Notes for Dissertation Integration

- Use this paper in the methodological literature review on AIS representation learning, maritime spatiotemporal modelling, and modality-specific encoders.
- Do not use it as direct evidence that shipping data improve Brent-price forecasts.
- Use it to justify why shipping data should not automatically be reduced to static aggregate indicators.
- Use its distinction between crude oil tankers and container ships to support tanker-specific PortWatch variables.
- Use its multi-branch feature-extraction design as an example of semantics-specific encoding.
- Adapt the port-call auxiliary task into tanker-flow, congestion, route-disruption, expected-arrival, or ETA-delay tasks.
- Replicate its modular ablation logic:
  - With versus without graph encoding.
  - With versus without auxiliary tasks.
  - Flat features versus representation learning.
- Combine structural intermediate outputs with SHAP:
  - SHAP explains engineered-feature contributions.
  - Auxiliary tasks explain the maritime state learned by the neural model.
  - Gating weights explain changes in modality importance over time.
- Apply strict time-based validation:
  - Rolling-window evaluation.
  - Expanding-window evaluation.
  - No random train–test split.
  - No future AIS or port-state information at the forecast origin.
- Keep the architecture proportional to the number of weekly observations.
- Consider pretraining the shipping encoder on high-frequency AIS data and freezing or partially freezing it during weekly Brent forecasting.
- Position the paper as methodological support for the distinction between:
  - **Core empirical layer**: Engineered M0–M4 variables with XGBoost, linear models, and TCN.
  - **Methodological contribution layer**: Modality-aware encoders and representation-level multimodal fusion.

---

## Suggested Dissertation Statement

TransES-ETA demonstrates that AIS trajectories need not be reduced to static, manually engineered indicators. By separately encoding spatial grids, within-grid vessel-movement patterns, irregular time intervals, departure ports, and vessel types, the framework preserves the semantic and spatiotemporal structure of maritime data. Its multi-task design further shows that an operationally meaningful auxiliary task—port-call prediction—can substantially improve both the predictive performance and structural interpretability of the main forecasting task. Although the study focuses on vessel ETA rather than commodity prices, it provides methodological support for constructing a modality-specific shipping encoder and introducing shipping-state auxiliary objectives in the proposed Brent forecasting framework.

## 核心方法

- **模型**：TransES-ETA，一种基于 Transformer 的端到端多任务学习框架。
- **主预测目标**：预测船舶抵达最终目的港的剩余时间，以小时为单位。
- **辅助预测目标**：预测船舶下一步到达的港口，包括中间挂靠港和最终目的港。
- **输入数据**：船舶自动识别系统 AIS 轨迹，包括空间位置、速度、航向、时间间隔、始发港和船舶类型。
- **数据表达方式**：将原始 AIS 轨迹划分为连续空间网格，构成嵌套序列：
  - 外层为不同空间网格组成的航线序列。
  - 内层为每个网格中的 AIS 观测集合。
- **空间编码**：采用基于 Transformer 的空间编码，将网格中心经纬度转换为高维向量。
- **局部模式编码器**：采用带残差连接的图卷积网络 GCNwRC，提取每个网格内部的局部 AIS 运动模式。
- **时间编码**：采用经过修改的正弦编码，表示 AIS 记录之间的不规则时间间隔。
- **类别编码**：使用可学习 embedding 表示始发港和船舶类型。
- **挂靠港预测分支**：使用平均池化和双向 GRU 预测下一港口，并生成航线相关的潜在特征。
- **ETA 预测分支**：Transformer Encoder 融合 AIS 表示和挂靠港表示，最后输出 ETA 回归结果。
- **特征重构**：Transformer Decoder 重构输入特征，以减少编码过程中的信息损失。
- **额外运营信息**：将港口周转时间或停港时间加入预测航行时间，形成最终 ETA。
- **训练方式**：联合优化港口分类损失、ETA 回归损失和特征重构损失，并动态调整多任务权重。
- **评价指标**：
  - 挂靠港预测使用 Accuracy。
  - ETA 预测使用 MAE。
  - ETA 预测使用 MSE。
  - 同时比较训练时间和推理时间。

## 语义数据转换

论文认为，原始 AIS 数据不能简单地作为普通等间隔时间序列处理，因为 AIS 数据包含：

- 不规则上报时间间隔。
- 缺失和噪声观测。
- 不同空间区域内数量不等的 AIS 点。
- 复杂的局部船舶运动。
- 整条航线中的长期依赖关系。

因此，作者将每条船舶轨迹划分为连续空间网格。

每个网格包含：

- 一个网格中心坐标。
- 一组长度不固定的 AIS 观测。
- 一个时间距离变量。
- 始发港和船舶类型信息。

最终形成如下嵌套结构：

```text
整条航程
├── 空间网格 1
│   ├── AIS 观测 1
│   ├── AIS 观测 2
│   └── AIS 观测 3
├── 空间网格 2
│   ├── AIS 观测 4
│   └── AIS 观测 5
└── 空间网格 N
```

外层网格序列表示长距离航线，内层 AIS 观测集合表示船舶在局部区域内的运动状态。

---

## GCNwRC 局部模式编码器

论文使用带残差连接的图卷积网络，替代先前研究中基于 GRU 的局部模式编码器。

在每个空间网格内部：

- 每条 AIS 观测被视为一个图节点。
- 网格中的 AIS 观测被构造成有向或双向完全图。
- 同一网格内的所有 AIS 节点相互连接。
- 不同网格的图在这一阶段不直接连接。
- 不同网格之间的关系由后续 Transformer 学习。

GCNwRC 模块包括：

- 两层图卷积。
- 两层 Batch Normalisation。
- ReLU 激活函数。
- 一层 Dropout。
- 从模块起点连接到终点的残差连接。

使用图结构的主要原因是提高计算效率。

如果对每个网格内的 AIS 序列分别使用 GRU，模型需要顺序处理不同网格，难以实现有效的批量并行计算。图表示则允许多个网格内的 AIS 点集同时处理。

---

## 挂靠港预测分支

辅助任务预测船舶下一步将到达的港口。

预测结果可以是：

- 中间挂靠港。
- 最终目的港。

该分支包括：

- 对多通道 AIS 特征进行平均池化。
- 使用双向 GRU 提取序列信息。
- 使用全连接分类层预测港口。
- 使用交叉熵损失训练。

Bi-GRU 输出分成两条路径：

1. 第一条路径用于下一港口分类。
2. 第二条路径将航线相关潜在特征传递给 ETA 分支。

因此，挂靠港任务具有两个作用：

- 提供具有运营含义的中间预测。
- 促使共享编码器学习有助于 ETA 预测的航线和港口操作信息。

---

## ETA 预测分支

ETA 分支接收两类信息：

1. 来自语义特征提取模块的 AIS 序列特征。
2. 来自辅助挂靠港预测分支的航线和港口特征。

特征融合后，Transformer Encoder 用于学习：

- 长期时间依赖。
- 不同空间网格之间的关系。
- 船舶运动、船型、始发港和挂靠港之间的交互。
- 普通 RNN 或 CNN 可能无法完整捕捉的全局关系。

全连接层将 Transformer 表示转换为：

```text
以小时为单位的剩余航行时间
```

最终预测结果为：

```text
最终 ETA
=
模型预测的剩余航行时间
+
港口停留时间
```

---

## 特征重构任务

Transformer Encoder 后连接一个 Transformer Decoder。

Decoder 尝试根据潜在表示重构原始融合输入特征。

重构目标能够促使 Encoder：

- 保留原始 AIS 输入中的重要信息。
- 避免只围绕 ETA 标签压缩表示。
- 学习更加完整和可迁移的特征。
- 减少编码过程中的信息损失。

因此，完整模型同时优化三个任务：

```text
1. 下一港口分类
2. ETA 回归
3. 输入特征重构
```

---

## 多任务损失

总损失定义为：

\[
L_{\text{total}}
=
\alpha L_{\text{port}}
+
0.001(0.66-\alpha)L_{\text{ETA}}
+
0.34L_{\text{reconstruction}}
\]

其中：

- \(L_{\text{port}}\) 为挂靠港分类损失。
- \(L_{\text{ETA}}\) 为 ETA 回归损失。
- \(L_{\text{reconstruction}}\) 为特征重构损失。
- \(0 < \alpha \leq 0.33\)。

由于 ETA 损失的初始数量级显著高于分类和重构损失，作者将 ETA 损失乘以 0.001，防止其完全主导训练过程。

模型还使用动态任务权重：

- 训练初期同时学习挂靠港预测和 ETA。
- 如果挂靠港预测损失连续 10 个 epoch 不再下降，则认为辅助任务已经基本收敛。
- 随后提高 ETA 任务的相对权重。
- 这样可以让辅助任务在训练早期帮助表示学习，同时避免其在后期持续限制主任务。

## 主要发现

1. **TransES-ETA 获得较好的 ETA 预测表现。**

   完整模型结果为：

   - MAE：约 2.46 小时。
   - MSE：约 14.51 小时²。

2. **挂靠港辅助任务显著改善 ETA 预测。**

   不加入挂靠港模块时：

   - MAE：5.39 小时。
   - MSE：74.38 小时²。

   加入挂靠港模块后：

   - MAE：2.46 小时。
   - MSE：14.51 小时²。

   相当于：

   - MAE 下降约 54.55%。
   - MSE 下降约 80.49%。

   MSE 的大幅下降表明，挂靠港任务不仅降低了平均预测误差，也减少了极端 ETA 预测错误。

3. **GCNwRC 优于原始 GRU 局部编码器。**

   原始 GRU 模型：

   - MAE：3.32 小时。
   - MSE：30.83 小时²。
   - 训练时间：215 分钟。
   - 单样本推理时间：0.0181 秒。

   改用 GCNwRC 后：

   - MAE：约 2.46 小时。
   - MSE：约 14.52 小时²。
   - 训练时间：41 分钟。
   - 单样本推理时间：0.0165 秒。

   改善幅度为：

   - MAE 下降 26.04%。
   - MSE 下降 52.90%。
   - 训练时间减少 80.93%。
   - 推理时间减少 8.84%。

4. **挂靠港任务提供结构性可解释性。**

   模型不只输出一个难以解释的 ETA 数值，还预测船舶下一步将到达的港口。

   挂靠港安排为最终到达时间提供了具有航运业务意义的中间解释。

5. **不同 AIS 信息适合使用不同编码器。**

   论文说明：

   - 经纬度可使用空间编码。
   - 不规则时间间隔可使用时间编码。
   - 港口和船型类别可使用 embedding。
   - 局部 AIS 点集可使用图神经网络。
   - 长距离轨迹依赖可使用 Transformer。

6. **港口周转时间包含额外预测信息。**

   显式考虑停港时间后，ETA 精度进一步提高。虽然挂靠港分支已经能够从 AIS 中隐式学习部分停港信息，但直接加入停港时间仍具有额外价值。

## 主要贡献

### 1. 保留 AIS 的结构化信息

论文没有将 AIS 轨迹压缩为平均航速、航程距离等少量静态变量，而是保留：

- 空间网格结构。
- 网格内部船舶运动。
- 不规则时间间隔。
- 港口和船型语义。
- 长距离航线依赖。

### 2. 使用语义专属编码器

不同类型的输入采用不同编码方式，而不是从一开始就作为同质数值变量直接拼接。

### 3. 多任务学习

挂靠港预测作为具有运营意义的辅助目标，改善了 ETA 主任务所使用的航运表示。

### 4. 结构性可解释性

下一挂靠港作为可解释的中间结果，将船舶航线行为与最终 ETA 联系起来。

### 5. 计算效率

用图结构进行并行处理，显著降低了局部 AIS 数据编码的训练时间。

## 对 M1–M4 的启示

### M1：市场基本面模型

该论文不会直接为 M1 提供金融变量或石油市场变量。

M1 应继续作为可解释的结构化基准，包括：

- Brent 和 WTI 历史价格。
- 原油库存。
- OVX 或 VIX。
- 汇率。
- 利率。
- 生产指标。
- 需求指标。

航运模型的价值应通过检验其是否能够在 M1 基础上提供增量预测能力来判断。

### M2：遥感扩展模型

论文支持“不同模态应使用适合自身结构的编码器”这一原则。

在本研究中：

- 卫星影像可使用 CNN、SatMAE、Prithvi 或其他地球观测编码器。
- 航运和 AIS 数据可使用图编码器和时序编码器。
- 金融时间序列可使用 TCN、GRU、TFT 或 Temporal Transformer。

各模态应先形成独立表示，再进行中期或后期融合。

### M3：航运扩展模型

该论文与 M3 的联系最直接。

它支持优先使用 tanker-specific PortWatch 变量，例如：

```text
pw_hormuz_n_tanker
pw_hormuz_capacity_tanker
pw_suez_n_tanker
pw_suez_capacity_tanker
pw_malacca_n_tanker
pw_malacca_capacity_tanker
```

而不是只依赖：

```text
total_vessel_count
total_vessel_capacity
```

它还说明 M3 不应局限于船舶数量和运力，还可以扩展：

- 平均油轮航速。
- 油轮航速异常。
- 锚地等待时间。
- 港口停留时间。
- 港口周转时间。
- ETA 延迟。
- 预计到港油轮数量。
- 航线偏离指标。
- 绕航比例。
- 起点—终点油轮流。
- 关键海峡拥堵程度。
- 挂靠港频率。
- 船型构成。

### M4：完整多模态模型

对于 M4，该论文支持如下表示级结构：

```text

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
```

## 可用于本研究的航运辅助任务

挂靠港辅助任务可以迁移为更贴近石油市场预测的目标。

可能的任务包括：

1. **下一周油轮流量预测**

   ```text
   历史航运状态
   →
   下一周 tanker count 或 tanker capacity
   ```

2. **关键海峡拥堵分类**

   ```text
   正常 / 中度拥堵 / 严重拥堵
   ```

3. **预计到港量预测**

   ```text
   未来七天到达主要原油进口港或出口港的
   油轮数量或运力
   ```

4. **航线中断分类**

   ```text
   正常航线 / 绕航 / 严重中断
   ```

5. **油轮延迟预测**

   ```text
   预计 ETA 延迟或 ETA 修订幅度
   ```

6. **航运状态分类**

   ```text
   低流量 / 正常流量 / 高流量状态
   ```

总损失可以写为：

\[
L_{\text{total}}
=
L_{\text{Brent}}
+
\lambda_1L_{\text{tanker flow}}
+
\lambda_2L_{\text{congestion}}
+
\lambda_3L_{\text{route disruption}}
\]

这些辅助任务可以促使航运编码器学习具有经济意义的物理供应信号，而不是只记忆历史样本中偶然存在的油价相关性。

## Shipping Encoder 的建议迁移方式

### 情况 A：只有 PortWatch 聚合数据

如果本研究使用的是日度或周度 PortWatch 指标，而不是单船 AIS 轨迹，则不应直接复制论文中的点级 GCNwRC。

更合适的结构为：

```text
每周关键海峡观测
[count, capacity, growth, anomaly, congestion]
        ↓
Chokepoint 或 Port Embedding
        ↓
航运网络图层
GCN / GAT
        ↓
时间层
TCN / GRU / Temporal Transformer
        ↓
每周航运表示 z_ship
```

图节点可以包括：

- 霍尔木兹海峡。
- 苏伊士运河。
- 曼德海峡。

## 局限性

1. **论文仍处于审稿阶段。**

   该文是 ICLR 2026 匿名投稿，并非已经正式接收的论文，因此其结果应被视为初步结论。

2. **地理泛化能力有限。**

   数据主要覆盖东亚港口至新加坡的航线，没有检验：

   - 霍尔木兹海峡航线。
   - 红海航线。
   - 苏伊士运河航线。
   - 欧洲航线。
   - 大西洋市场航线。
   - 美国墨西哥湾沿岸航线。

3. **只有一个最终目的港。**

   所有航程均以新加坡为终点，目的地结构较为简单，可能限制模型在全球航运网络中的可迁移性。

4. **船舶类别有限。**

   数据包括原油油轮和集装箱船，但不包括：

   - 成品油轮。
   - LNG 船。
   - LPG 船。
   - 干散货船。
   - 化学品船。

5. **不能直接证明 AIS 能够改善油价预测。**

   论文预测的是船舶 ETA，而不是 Brent 或 WTI，因此不能证明其 AIS 表示一定能够提高商品价格预测表现。

6. **样本外设计可能不够严格。**

   论文报告 90%/10% 的训练测试划分，但没有明确说明该划分是：

   - 按时间划分。
   - 随机划分。
   - 按船舶独立划分。
   - 按航线独立划分。
   - 按港口独立划分。

   如果同一航程的重叠滑动窗口同时出现在训练集和测试集中，则结果可能高估真实泛化能力。

7. **缺少数据规模细节。**

   论文没有明确报告：

   - 航程总数。
   - AIS 消息总数。
   - 滑动窗口样本总数。
   - 独立船舶数量。

8. **跨论文 benchmark 不完全可比。**

   不同研究使用的数据集、船型、航线和评价方式不同，因此更低的 MAE 不一定意味着模型具有普遍优势。

9. **港口周转时间部分在模型外处理。**

   停港时间在 ETA 回归之后加入，而不是作为统一模型输入或单独预测任务处理。

10. **可解释性仍然有限。**

    下一港口预测提供了结构性解释，但仍然不能完整说明：

    - 哪些 AIS 观测最重要。
    - 哪些空间网格最重要。
    - 航速、航向和绕航如何影响 ETA。
    - 学习到的关系是否具有因果意义。

11. **缺少同一数据集上的简单基准模型比较。**

    论文没有系统比较：

    - 线性回归。
    - 随机森林。
    - XGBoost。
    - 历史平均 ETA。
    - 航线平均 ETA。

12. **对周度油价数据可能过于复杂。**

    本研究的周度 Brent 数据可能只有数百至约一千个样本。直接在该数据上训练 GCN–Bi-GRU–Transformer–Decoder，可能导致严重过拟合。

## 在本论文中的整合方式

- 将该文放入 AIS 表示学习、海事时空建模和模态专属编码器的方法学文献综述。
- 不将其作为“航运数据能够预测 Brent”的直接实证证据。
- 用其解释为什么航运数据不应必然被压缩为静态汇总指标。
- 用其对原油油轮和集装箱船的区分，支持 tanker-specific PortWatch 变量。
- 将其多分支特征提取结构作为语义专属编码的案例。
- 将挂靠港辅助任务转换为油轮流量、拥堵、航线中断、预计到港量或 ETA 延迟任务。
- 借鉴其模块化消融设计：
  - 有无图编码。
  - 有无辅助任务。
  - 扁平特征与表示学习。
- 将结构性中间输出与 SHAP 结合：
  - SHAP 解释工程特征贡献。
  - 辅助任务解释神经网络学习到的航运状态。
  - 门控权重解释不同时间的模态重要性。
- 对油价预测采用严格的时间验证：
  - Rolling-window evaluation。
  - Expanding-window evaluation。
  - 不使用随机训练测试划分。
  - 预测时不得使用未来 AIS 或未来港口状态。
- 控制模型规模，使其与周度样本量相匹配。
- 可以先在高频 AIS 数据上预训练 Shipping Encoder，再在周度 Brent 预测中冻结或部分冻结。
- 将该论文用于支持本研究的两层设计：
  - **核心实证层**：工程化 M0–M4 变量，结合 XGBoost、线性模型和 TCN。
  - **方法创新层**：模态感知编码器与表示级多模态融合。

## 可直接用于论文的英文表述

TransES-ETA demonstrates that AIS trajectories need not be reduced to static, manually engineered indicators. By separately encoding spatial grids, within-grid vessel-movement patterns, irregular time intervals, departure ports, and vessel types, the framework preserves the semantic and spatiotemporal structure of maritime data. Its multi-task design further shows that an operationally meaningful auxiliary task—port-call prediction—can substantially improve both the predictive performance and structural interpretability of the main forecasting task. Although the study focuses on vessel ETA rather than commodity prices, it provides methodological support for constructing a modality-specific shipping encoder and introducing shipping-state auxiliary objectives in the proposed Brent forecasting framework.

## 可直接用于论文的中文表述

TransES-ETA 表明，AIS 轨迹不应必然被压缩为静态人工统计指标。该模型分别编码空间网格、网格内船舶运动模式、不规则时间间隔、始发港和船舶类型，从而保留航运数据的语义与时空结构。其多任务设计进一步说明，具有业务含义的辅助任务——挂靠港预测——能够显著改善主任务的预测精度和结构性可解释性。尽管该研究的预测目标是船舶 ETA，而非石油价格，但它为本研究构建模态专属航运编码器，并在 Brent 预测框架中引入航运状态辅助目标，提供了直接的方法学依据。
